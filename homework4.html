Penetration scores and random processes.

This study models the score accumulated by attackers across a collection of systems. It compares Bernoulli increments, which represent non-negative successful steps, with symmetric Rademacher increments, which represent gains and losses. Simulations illustrate how totals, averages, and normalized totals behave as the system size increases.

03 / Mathematical model

What the theory predicts

Let X₁, …, Xₙ be independent and identically distributed increments, and let Sₙ = ΣXᵢ.

Bernoulli model

P(Xᵢ = 1) = p,   P(Xᵢ = 0) = 1 − p
E[Xᵢ] = p
Var(Xᵢ) = p(1 − p)
  • Total: E[Sₙ] = np and Var(Sₙ) = np(1 − p).
  • Average: E[Sₙ/n] = p and Var(Sₙ/n) = p(1 − p)/n.
  • Normalized total: E[Sₙ/√n] = p√n and Var(Sₙ/√n) = p(1 − p).

The average converges in probability to p. The uncentered Sₙ/√n does not converge to a standard normal when p > 0 because its mean grows like p√n. A centered CLT normalization is (Sₙ − np)/√(np(1 − p)) when 0 < p < 1.

Rademacher model

P(Xᵢ = +1) = P(Xᵢ = −1) = 1/2
E[Xᵢ] = 0
Var(Xᵢ) = 1
  • Total: E[Sₙ] = 0 and Var(Sₙ) = n.
  • Average: E[Sₙ/n] = 0 and Var(Sₙ/n) = 1/n.
  • Normalized total: E[Sₙ/√n] = 0 and Var(Sₙ/√n) = 1.

The Law of Large Numbers gives Sₙ/n → 0 in probability. By the Central Limit Theorem, Sₙ/√n converges in distribution to N(0,1). Its variance stays equal to one, so the normalized distribution does not collapse to a point.

04 / Cybersecurity interpretation

What n and m mean

System size n

  • In the Bernoulli model, every step adds either zero or one. The total therefore grows around np as more systems are considered.
  • In the Rademacher model, positive and negative increments balance on average. The total fluctuates around zero, with a typical magnitude on the order of √n rather than n.
  • The average score becomes more stable as n increases. This is the simulation’s practical illustration of the Law of Large Numbers.
  • For Rademacher scores, dividing by √n keeps the variance at one and reveals the bell-shaped limiting distribution predicted by the Central Limit Theorem.

Number of attackers m

  • The page treats each attacker as an independent trajectory across n systems.
  • Increasing m does not change the theoretical distribution for one attacker. It gives more observations, usually making the histogram and empirical moments more stable.
  • If all attackers’ scores are added, Aₙ,ₘ = Σⱼ₌₁ᵐ Sₙ⁽ʲ⁾, then E[Aₙ,ₘ] = mnμ and Var(Aₙ,ₘ) = mnσ², assuming independence.
  • A larger m can increase the largest score observed simply because there are more attackers in the sample. This does not mean that any one attacker became more effective.
Model limitation. Real penetration attempts are not necessarily independent or identically distributed. Attackers may share information or resources, systems can have different vulnerabilities, and defenders may adapt after an incident. Bernoulli and Rademacher increments are simplified models for studying probability laws, not complete operational predictions of a real network.

Conclusion

The simulations demonstrate three different views of accumulated random scores. The total retains the accumulated scale and has variance proportional to n. The average removes that scale and concentrates around the expected increment by the Law of Large Numbers. The square-root normalization preserves a non-degenerate spread; in the zero-mean, unit-variance Rademacher case, the normalized total approaches the standard normal distribution by the Central Limit Theorem. The number of attackers m mainly controls the size of the simulated sample and, when scores are aggregated, the scale of the combined total.
